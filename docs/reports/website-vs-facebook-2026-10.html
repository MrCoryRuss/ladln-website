The good news

  • Donate is the most clicked thing on the site. 8 of 33 clicks. 5 visitors made it all the way into the Zeffy checkout.
  • The Facebook card works. It is the most clicked content block below the banner, and 7 visitors clicked out to an external link, mostly Facebook.

  • Returning visitors are 42%. A small, loyal group checks back.

4. What the data says we should do

The website's job is narrow and it is doing it: be the credible, official landing spot when someone wants to donate, join, or verify we are real. Donate is the most clicked element on the site. Keep it clean, fast, and current. Put the storytelling effort where the audience is.

Worth doing (low effort, real payoff)

  • Post student and graduate photos to Facebook first. The website's Facebook feed republishes them automatically. One upload, both places.
  • Move a short "Meet our graduates" strip up the homepage to just under the stat tiles. That takes the photos from being seen by 17% of visitors to 100%. One-time change, about an hour of work.
  • Fix the Facebook feed box. It throws an error on some phones and may show blank. A third of visitors are on phones.
  • Tag links we put in emails and newsletters so we can see which outreach actually drives visits. Right now 60% of traffic has no known source.
  • Keep Donate and Membership one click from the top. Already true; protect it.

Hold off (effort will not be seen)

  • Building out a large photo gallery on the website. Fewer than 1 in 5 visitors scroll to where it would sit. The audience for it is on Facebook, and a short strip near the top covers the website side.
  • Adding more sections to the homepage. People already stop a third of the way down. Longer makes it worse.
  • Weekly website content updates. With 2 to 3 visits a day, nobody is checking back for news. Facebook has 1,100 followers who are.
  • Investing in search (SEO). Google sends 2 visitors a week. Fine to leave as is.
